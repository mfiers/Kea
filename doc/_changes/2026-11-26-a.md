## Asked

Mark Fiers, 25 and 26 November 2026: the cycle takes too long (a question is signed, then its steps are discussed and signed, run, the results signed, then the conclusions), how do we make it faster? After a critical read by Fable (a second model): three steps instead: first the owner says in words what the experiment should do and which questions it asks; then Claude formalises and builds the experiment, questions, hypotheses and steps; the owner signs that off; and last comes the execution. Decisions: the review of results and conclusions afterwards holds back only the pages that rely on them; the brief may come in chat or on the site; one design round covers the whole experiment; the new pace is the default, `guided` stays available.

## Found

Measured on trajectories (six cycles; commit times as a proxy): the agent needed 2 to 37 minutes per cycle; the waits between cycles were 19 minutes to 19 hours, once 4 days. Each question passed four round trips (question, plan, results, conclusions), each with a Harvest and an Implement press: about eight waits for the owner per question. Four of the five items of cycle 6 only said "signed, so next stage". Two of the four gates are ceremony (signing a question before its hypotheses may be drafted; signing results before a conclusion may be drafted). The one that carries the science, fixing hypotheses before any data, was the least enforced: the build checked that a step was signed, not that the hypothesis it tests was signed, nor that the hypothesis was committed before its result.

## Changed

- New `pace: design` (kea.yaml; the default of `kea init`). The design round may write questions, hypotheses and steps of the whole experiment together; conclusions may be drafted in the same round as their results. A step in progress or done is a problem unless its design is signed: the step, every page above it up to the experiment, the hypotheses it tests and what any of these relies on (premises, decisions, results, conclusions). So the review of a conclusion holds back only a step that relies on it.
- `kea context` under design names the round to do or the pages that wait: the design from the brief (the brief word for word under `## Brief` on the experiment page; data described, outcomes unseen), the design's sign-off, the execution round (every step whose design is signed; a failed or changed step stops its own question only), the review.
- Progress explains the two rounds; Claude's next steps list a planned step only when its design is signed. The question routes and the sign order (Approve & next, n on the graph) work as before, with the design pages signable together.
- Under every pace, a hypothesis with `fixed_before_results: true` must have been added in an earlier commit than any result that tests it (git history order; the same commit is not earlier): a problem under design, a note under guided and free.
- The skill, the CLAUDE.md section of `kea init`, Help (How a project progresses, What is Kea) and the "Propose ▸ an experiment" instruction (the comment is the brief) describe the new pace; `guided` is described as before.

A review of the change (a second agent, Fable) found holes that are closed here:
- A result could appear with its step still planned, or with no step at all.
- A step that names no hypothesis skipped its question's hypotheses.
- Premises and decisions not linked by `relies_on` did not hold a step back.
- A conclusion relied on did not bring the results it rests on.
- A shallow clone or a move of a file out of doc/ and back gave a false "committed no earlier" problem.
- A question's run showed as done while its steps waited on a review.
- The harvest would have planned steps that may not run yet.
- Nothing marked a conclusion as a draft.

Now: the design of a step is its whole question (every live hypothesis, the premises and decisions of the experiment, of the question and above the step, the decisions applied in it) plus what these rely on and, for a conclusion, its evidence. A step that is done is held to what it names itself and to the rest of its design as committed before its first result, so a decision written after the run (a deviation) does not fault it. A result needs a step that ran on a signed design. The commit order uses git's order of commits (parents first), follows moves before reporting, and skips a shallow clone with a note. A conclusion nobody has reviewed shows "draft · not reviewed", and a question's answer that rests on one is labelled "Draft answer". A hypothesis whose prediction changes after its result was already a build problem (propagation.py).

Hub: upgrade not needed (build rules, context, help and page text; no endpoint, status field, event or publish format changed). Existing projects keep their `pace:`; to switch, set `pace: design` in kea.yaml and refresh the CLAUDE.md section.

## Checked

`pytest` (161 passed; tests/test_design_pace.py new, 13 tests: design drafted in one round without problems, the sign order top down, a step runs only on a signed design, a run on an unsigned design is a problem naming the unsigned pages, the review holds back only a step relying on an unreviewed conclusion, the brief as first step, the commit order of fixed hypotheses with git, and each hole the review found: a result without a signed step, a step without tests:, unlinked premises and decisions, a conclusion relied on with an unsigned result, a decision written after the run, a shallow clone, a move out of doc/ and back, the draft labels). The `kea init` example now writes `pace: design`. A clone of trajectories checks and builds with 0 problems under both paces. Under design, `kea context` names the pages each question waits for, 17 pages carry the draft label, and three questions show a draft answer.
