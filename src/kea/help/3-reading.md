## Reading a Kea site

Start at the top. With one experiment the site opens at the experiment's page: its summary, then every question with its answer in short. With more experiments the project page summarises them first. A question page leads with its answer so far, then its conclusions, results and hypotheses. Details are one click further down.

Every page has a type, shown as a coloured tag with an icon and a number (Q1, R2, C3; references show author and year). The tag is the link to the page; hover it for the page's one-line summary. In lists of pages the whole line is the link.

<!-- KINDS -->

| Type | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Experiment | the experiment's home page: its summary, then every question with its answer in short |
| Premise | what the analysis takes as given, so a reader can question it |
| Question | what is asked; leads with its answer so far and the conclusions behind it |
| Hypothesis | what was expected, with its prediction and criterion, and whether it was written before its test |
| Step | what was done (the methods: data, tool, version, test, output files) |
| Decision | a choice made along the way, why, and the alternatives |
| Result | what a file shows; every number comes from a named file |
| Conclusion | what the results mean, with the evidence for and against and a certainty |
| Figure | a figure with its full caption, script and table |
| Artefact | a data file the analysis made, with checksum, preview and download |
| Resource | what the analysis takes from outside (a database, dataset, file or tool), with version, retrieval date and checksum |
| Caveat | what limits a result, conclusion or premise; open until a step addresses it |
| Term | a glossary entry; words with a dotted underline link to it |
| Reference | a paper, with its abstract and why it is cited |

On a page:

- The coloured bar on the left and the word above the title say the type. The page's own content (title, summary, text) is set in a typewriter face, indented behind a line. After it, tabs hang under one thick line in the type's colour; one is open at a time. "About this page" holds what Kea knows about the page: its permanent identifier (`kea:…`, which stays when the page is edited or moved, and `@…` for exactly this version, to cite), its sources, and its links to other pages as tables ("Tested by this step", "Supported by these results"), with the reason each link holds; long tables come in pages of 12. "Changes" lists one line per version (release), each with "What changed on this page": the words taken out struck through and the words put in underlined, for this page alone (the newest open); "Comments" the comments when the site was built. Resources, terms and references are not listed on the pages: the menu has them under Map.
- A page reads in this order: its title, summary and text, as written; on a question, the answer so far; then its figures and data files under their own separator (one tab per figure or file when there are several; the take-home under each figure, the rest of the caption under "Shows, method and statistics"; a question or conclusion also shows the figures of the results behind it as a row of small images); then the tabs. On an experiment page the first tabs are "Questions" (one line each) and "Answers so far" (each answer, with links to its conclusions), and "About this page" lists its hypotheses, steps, results and conclusions with a filter per kind. Under About, conclusions and caveats are listed one per line; how the page was made (steps, data, sources) is folded under Provenance; the exact changes per version are under History.
- Pills next to the title: the status (green: supported, done or answered; amber: open, planned, assumed or inconclusive; dark red: not supported, deprecated or dropped); "exploratory"; on a hypothesis "written before its test" or "written after results"; on a conclusion its certainty; "waiting for approval", "approved", "confirmed", "recheck"; and the release in which the page last changed.
- The menu bar: the kea head goes home, then project / experiment / page. Map (the experiment as a tree: the questions first, each folded to one line, then premises and decisions; resources, terms and references under headings of their own, folded) opens, in a light strip beside it, Relations (the graph of links), Objects (every page in one table to search, filter and sort), Resources, Terms, References, Data files and Code; Plan (the current review cycle; Current comments); History (what changed per agent run; review cycles; all comments); PDF (the analysis as one document to read; with all records under it); Help. The signed-in name opens a menu with your comments, help and sign-out, and at its foot the Kea versions of the site and of the server or hub.
- The graph (Graph in the menu bar, or g g on any page, which opens it at that page). Every page of the site on one sheet, left to right along the route of an experiment: a column per stage (questions, hypotheses, steps, results, figures, conclusions, and last what sits beside them: caveats, decisions, premises, artefacts), a band per question, and the experiment as a dark bar on the left that holds its bands. Each page is a box with its tag in its type's colour (filled when signed, outlined when not) and, under its title, its kind and scientific state: a hypothesis fixed before results (or written after), an exploratory or confirmatory analysis, a status, a conclusion's certainty; ✓ signed, an amber dot when it waits for your sign-off, ! when a page it rests on changed since it was signed. Lines show what grows from what (a step from the hypothesis it tests, a result from the step that made it, a figure from its result); green lines carry the evidence for a conclusion, red dashed the evidence against. The selected page lights up its chain of reasoning: in blue what it rests on, in orange what rests on it, followed along the route (a decision or caveat is shown, not followed further). The box at the bottom left says the same in words (made by S1 · tests H1 · supports C1 · limited by W1). Keys: → (or d) goes to what follows from the page (a question's hypotheses, the step that tests a hypothesis, the result it made, the conclusion it supports; it remembers where you came from), ← (or a) back to what it rests on, ↑ ↓ (or w s) the page above or below in the same stage, PgUp PgDn the previous or next question; Space, Enter or a click opens the page in place: it zooms out of its box and you read, comment and sign as on the page itself (a, c and the other page keys work there); Esc closes what is open on the page first (the comment box, a menu) and then zooms back into the graph, at the same place (an unsent comment asks for a second Esc). n and p go to the next and previous page that waits for a sign-off, / finds a page by its tag or words of its title, o folds a question's band to one line with its counts (O keeps only this question open), f shows the whole graph on the screen, ? lists the keys. The small overview in the bottom right corner shows where you are on the whole sheet; click it to go there. The address keeps the page you are on (explore.html#experiment:page), so a link opens the graph there; the pages themselves stay where they were.
- Where you are. Every page of an experiment belongs to a question: its own, the question it sits under, or, for a caveat, decision or premise, the question of the pages it is linked with (a page shared by most questions belongs to the experiment as a whole). The menu bar shows that question's tag before the page's (Q1 › R1; hover for the question; +1 when the page serves another question too), the breadcrumbs name it in full and end with the page's tag, and the coloured bar on the left carries it at its top. Under the breadcrumbs a strip shows the question's route: its hypotheses, steps, results, figures and conclusions, then what sits beside it (caveats, decisions, premises), each a tag you can click, filled when signed, outlined when not, ringed for this page, with where you are on the right (R1 · 4 of 9 · 1 not signed); a kind with more than eight pages folds to a count, and on a phone every kind does. In the top right corner (at the end of the strip when the window is narrow) a small plan of the experiment: one column per question, one row per stage, one dot per page, amber for the pages that need your look this round, a yellow ring for this page that slides from the page you came from. Hover a dot for its page, click it to go there; click elsewhere on it, or press **m**, for the whole plan with every page's tag (arrows move, Enter opens, Esc or m closes).
- Keyboard, outside a text field. Three menus mirror the menu bar, the page's buttons and the round's buttons. **g** opens a menu of places in three parts. Small letters go to places on the site, in the menu bar's order: g s Start, g e the experiment's front page, g u the page above, g a the first page that needs attention, g r Progress, g p Plan, g c Current comments, g m Map, g l Relations, g o Objects, g t Terms, g b References, g d Data files, g k Code, g w what changed, g h review cycles, g f the PDF. Capitals go to the pages linked to this one, by the letter of their number as the page shows it: g Q its question, g H hypothesis, g S step, g R result, g F figure, g C conclusion, g W caveat, g P premise, g D decision, g A artefact, g O resource, g T term, g B reference; the menu lists only the kinds this page links to (in its relations, either direction, or in its text), one page opens at once and several open a numbered choice (g R then 2). A number goes to that page of this page's kind in the experiment (g 3 on a question: Q3; g 1 2 for Q12; on the experiment's front page, its questions), and **←** / **→** (with or without g) go to the previous and next page of the same kind (Q2 ← Q3 → Q4). **m** opens the experiment's plan (above). **s** opens the site's actions: s h Harvest, s i Implement (only on the plan page, below the plan, as its button), s x cancel a harvest or implement request that has not started; each asks first, and Enter (or y) confirms, Esc (or n) does not. **a** opens this page's actions, grouped as its buttons are: sign-off (a a approve this page, a n approve and open the next page that needs attention, a d take back your approve before the Harvest), change (a t edit the title, a s edit the summary, a p edit the whole page, a r revise, a x drop) and more (a c comment, with the cursor in the comment box, a + propose a next step, a w work for the next run, a m manuscript). **n** and **p** go to the next and previous page in the reading order, top down through the experiment, always the same; **N** and **P** do the same but stop only at the pages of this round's attention list, including the ones you approved since the last Harvest. After each of these jumps a thin bar under the menu bar shows the order for a moment: one segment per page, amber for needs attention, green for approved this round, grey for nothing to do, with the page you are on raised. c opens the comments with the cursor in the comment box (Esc closes them); ] and [ (or 1 to 9) switch the tabs below the text and bring them up under the menu bar; ? lists every key. A key signs at most the page you are on, and like a click nothing starts: your sign-off waits for the Harvest. Only s h and s i (each confirmed) harvest or implement. Hovering over a button or a menu-bar link shows its key first (g p · Plan …, a n · Sign off …). With text selected, the popup has its own keys, shown on each button: c comment, e edit, and one letter per tag (x explain, s shorten, n needs source …); while it is open, a single key belongs to it. In a comment field Ctrl+Enter (⌘+Enter) sends and Esc cancels.
